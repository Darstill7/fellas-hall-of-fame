For eight weeks, Mitch had everything: the league's best record, its highest-scoring offense and an average of nearly 129 points per game. Then Stover beat him, 102.58–97.76, despite leaving an inactive player in his lineup for the second straight Sunday.

The kicker? Stover made a roster move that morning. He was online. He knew football was happening. Apparently, filling out an entire lineup is a personal choice.

That's The Fellas in a nutshell. Ten years of studying matchups and chasing waiver claims, only to discover that the best team can lose to a man treating roster management like a voluntary workplace survey. Now Week 10 brings a heavyweight showdown, a genuine record chase and an increasingly desperate playoff bubble.

01 · The main event

The favorite is wounded.

Mitch (8–1) vs. Stew (6–3)

MITCH -3.5TOTAL 244.5

Mitch leads Stew 10–6 in their 16 archived regular-season meetings. Their Week 10 meeting features the league's two highest-scoring teams, with a combined 2,247.46 points through nine weeks.

Stew's route to an upset starts with Drake Maye and Jahmyr Gibbs, while Mitch counters with Josh Allen, Jonathan Taylor and Jaxon Smith-Njigba. Smith-Njigba's steady target volume gives Mitch a reliable edge in what could be a tight game.

But there's history beyond the rivalry. Stew scored at least 105 points in each of his first eight games before falling to 87.36 last week. Mitch owns the longer benchmark: 12 straight at 105-plus in 2025. He's also one weekly scoring crown away from matching his own completed-season record of five.

The swing player: Drake London. If London delivers a big game, Mitch can survive even a monster outing from Gibbs.

THE PICK: MITCH 124, STEW 121.
02 · The playoff squeeze

Three superheroes. One ordinary record.

Stilly (5–4) vs. Scott (4–5)

STILLY -13.5TOTAL 223.5

Ja'Marr Chase. CeeDee Lamb. Amon-Ra St. Brown. Three elite receivers on one roster—and Harry Woody is somehow 5–4. Stilly built the Avengers, then equipped them with a backfield resembling the cleanup crew after the movie.

Josh Jacobs is listed out, and Woody Marks carries a knee concern. That puts the burden on Tyler Allgeier and Ollie Gordon II to deliver something useful alongside Stilly's receivers. Scott has the opposite construction: Bijan Robinson and Ashton Jeanty anchor his lineup, but Scott's team has averaged just 100.11 points per week.

The swing player: St. Brown. His rib designation is the major variable. A healthy St. Brown makes the receiving gap daunting; a limited one gives Scott's running backs a chance.

Stilly leads their archived meetings 3–2. A third straight win would create meaningful playoff breathing room. Frankly, given his receiving corps, it's about damn time.

THE PICK: STILLY 119, SCOTT 105.
03 · The history chase

One more week for immortality.

JMid (4–5) vs. Thommy (3–6)

JMID -15.5TOTAL 217.5

Evans won three straight weekly scoring titles in 2016. Nobody has surpassed that run in the recovered Fellas records. JMid has scored 141.68 and 148.74 in consecutive weeks, putting him one scoring crown away from matching it.

He's also 4–5. Fantasy football is a stupid fucking game sometimes.

Joe Burrow, Kenneth Walker III and George Kittle give JMid several ways to keep the surge alive. Thommy, meanwhile, is 3–6 after losing by just 0.76 last week—and the injury to Puka Nacua has stripped his offense of a player who could single-handedly reverse a bad Sunday.

The swing player: Kenneth Walker III. His workload and scoring opportunities give JMid another potential anchor. Thommy needs a monster performance from Tee Higgins or Malik Nabers to answer.

Thommy leads their archived meetings 2–1, but history offers no replacement for Nacua. A third JMid scoring title would tie the researched consecutive-title mark; a fourth next week would break it, pending final record-book certification.

THE PICK: JMID 117, THOMMY 101.
04 · The winless watch

One goddamn victory. That's the assignment.

Tony (6–3) vs. Kuhl (0–9)

TONY -21.5TOTAL 203.5

Tony has won 10 of his 12 archived meetings against Kuhl, who's averaging just 85.21 points and has been outscored by almost 365 through nine weeks. At some point, this stops resembling a bad season and starts looking like a hostage situation.

Justin Jefferson, Chris Olave and Brock Bowers give Tony a substantial talent advantage, but the lineup isn't immune to an ordinary week. Kuhl still has playmakers in Nico Collins, A.J. Brown and Ladd McConkey—enough that an upset is not mathematically impossible.

The swing player: A.J. Brown. One monster receiving performance could make Tony sweat, particularly if his biggest names post merely adequate scores.

Of course, the consequences for Tony would be catastrophic. Imagine chasing a first-round bye and becoming the first manager to lose to 0–9 Kuhl. The group chat would never grant parole.

THE PICK: TONY 113, KUHL 91.
05 · The wild-card mess

Preparation is apparently optional.

Birch (5–4) vs. Stover (4–5)

BIRCH -2.5TOTAL 226.5

Birch owns a 10–3 record against Stover in their archived regular-season meetings. That's less a rivalry than a recurring appointment. But Stover just beat previously unbeaten Mitch with an incomplete lineup, so the usual logic deserves a short vacation.

Christian McCaffrey and Kyren Williams give Stover an explosive backfield. Birch answers with Derrick Henry, James Cook, Patrick Mahomes and Rashee Rice. The matchup may hinge on which backfield turns its opportunities into touchdowns.

The swing player: Kyren Williams. A heavy rushing and red-zone workload could turn Stover from underdog into favorite. That assumes, naturally, that Stover actually starts a defense.

His defensive slot was empty in the latest lineup snapshot. Again. After last week, perhaps the rest of the league should stop wasting time filling theirs.

THE PICK: BIRCH 115, STOVER 112.

The closing column

The Final Flush

Mitch wants redemption. Stew wants a statement. JMid can tie a decade-old record, while Stilly wants his absurd roster to resemble a genuine contender.

Tony is chasing a bye. Birch and Stover are fighting for position. Thommy and Scott are fighting for survival.

And Kuhl is fighting for his fucking life.

Ten years have taught us that talent, preparation and every last point matter. They've also taught us that a guy who can't fill out his lineup can beat the best team in the league.

Study your projections. Check the injuries. Make your decisions. Somewhere Sunday, somebody will do something catastrophically stupid, and the other nine will spend the next decade reminding him.

That's The Fellas.

The football ends Monday night. The bullshit never does.
