Stover logged into Sleeper on Sunday morning, made a roster move and still left an inactive player in his lineup. Naturally, he beat the best team in the league.

Mitch spent nine weeks building the league's strongest statistical résumé. Stover apparently spent nine minutes confirming his phone still worked. Final score: 102.58–97.76.

Ten years into The Fellas, there's still no reliable explanation for how any of this shit works. Now the league leader faces Stew in a rematch of a genuinely historic shootout, JMid has a shot at a decade-old record, and Kuhl is a loss away from double-digit disgrace.

01 · The main event

Last time, they nearly broke the scoreboard.

Mitch (8–1) vs. Stew (6–3)

STEW -1.5TOTAL 242.5

Last season, Mitch beat Stew 167.82–164.80. Their combined 332.62 points remain the highest single-game total in the recovered Fellas regular-season archive. This year's Week 1 rematch? Another 319.78 points. These two apparently believe defense is a rumor.

Now the league's top two offenses meet again, only Mitch has just suffered his first loss—to Stover, who didn't even start an active player at every position.

Stew counters with Drake Maye and Jahmyr Gibbs, projected for roughly 22 points apiece. Mitch has Josh Allen, the week's leading projected quarterback, but Seattle's decision to start Drew Lock adds uncertainty to Jaxon Smith-Njigba's ceiling.

The number that matters: Independent starter projections put Stew ahead 122.0–120.5. After years of Mitch tormenting him—10 wins in 16 meetings—Stew has his opening.

THE PICK: STEW 122, MITCH 121.
02 · The playoff squeeze

The Avengers are still 5–4.

Stilly (5–4) vs. Scott (4–5)

STILLY -19.5TOTAL 228.5

Ja'Marr Chase, CeeDee Lamb and Amon-Ra St. Brown share a fantasy roster. And Harry Woody, a team apparently assembled through some clerical oversight, is just 5–4.

The receiving room belongs in a museum. The backfield belongs on a missing-persons flyer. Josh Jacobs is out, leaving Ollie Gordon II, Tyler Allgeier and a banged-up Woody Marks to keep the offense upright.

Scott brings the opposite roster: Bijan Robinson and Ashton Jeanty offer a legitimate backfield, but his team has averaged only 100.11 points per game. Week 10's starter projections favor Stilly about 124–105.

The catch: St. Brown's rib issue could eat into that advantage. Scott also needs his recently added Corey Kiner to contribute if he's going to keep pace.

With that much talent, Stilly shouldn't need divine intervention to reach 6–4. But he'd probably take it.

THE PICK: STILLY 124, SCOTT 105.
03 · The history chase

The hottest 4–5 team alive.

JMid (4–5) vs. Thommy (3–6)

JMID -16.5TOTAL 216.5

In 2016, Evans won three consecutive weekly scoring crowns. Nobody has surpassed it in the reconstructed league history. JMid has two straight after posting 141.68 and 148.74, giving him one more shot at the mark.

He's also 4–5. This is what happens when fantasy football decides to operate without adult supervision.

Joe Burrow and Kenneth Walker III each project for about 20 points, with George Kittle another premium weapon. Thommy, meanwhile, is 3–6, fresh off a 0.76-point defeat, and without injured Puka Nacua. That's less a playoff push than a crime scene.

The historical stakes: Thommy actually leads their rivalry 2–1. But the present projection favors JMid 116.3–99.9. One more league-high score puts his name beside Evans; two more put him alone.

THE PICK: JMID 116, THOMMY 100.
04 · The winless watch

One goddamn victory. That's all.

Tony (6–3) vs. Kuhl (0–9)

TONY -29.5TOTAL 207.5

Kuhl is 0–9, averaging 85.21 points, and has been outscored by nearly 365 this year. Tony has beaten him in 10 of their 12 historical meetings. The league office may need to send a wellness check instead of a matchup reminder.

The refreshed lineup model is even less charitable: Tony 118.6, Kuhl 88.7. Dak Prescott and Tony's receiving depth are projected to do the damage, while Kuhl needs unexpected explosions from Nico Collins, A.J. Brown or Ladd McConkey.

The real stakes: Tony is chasing a bye. Beat the winless team and nobody says a word. Lose to Kuhl and the group chat will bring it up at your funeral.

THE PICK: TONY 119, KUHL 89.
05 · The wild-card mess

He picked up a defense. Will he start it?

Birch (5–4) vs. Stover (4–5)

BIRCH -14.5TOTAL 223.5

Birch leads this rivalry 10–3. Last week, Stover beat undefeated Mitch despite starting an inactive player. That combination should tell you everything you need to know about the laws governing this league.

Christian McCaffrey and Kyren Williams give Stover a credible path to winning. Birch answers with James Cook, Derrick Henry, Patrick Mahomes and Rashee Rice. On the active-lineup snapshot, those nine starters project to 118.7 points for Birch and 104.4 for Stover.

Here's the catch: Stover actually picked up the Rams defense, but at the latest lineup check, they were still on his bench and his starting defense slot was empty. The Rams project for roughly ten points—points he'll only get if he remembers to start them. The pickup was sensible. Whether he follows through is the entertainment.

The hinge: A filled defense slot would make this a much tighter game. Until he sets that final slot, Birch has the edge. After last week, nobody should assume Stover's lineup is final before kickoff.

THE PICK: BIRCH 119, STOVER 104 (AS CURRENTLY SET).

The closing column

The Final Flush

Mitch wants redemption. Stew wants a statement. JMid can tie a decade-old record, while Stilly wants his absurd roster to resemble a genuine contender.

Tony is chasing a bye. Birch and Stover are fighting for position. Thommy and Scott are fighting for survival.

And Kuhl is fighting for his fucking life.

Ten years have taught us that talent, preparation and every last point matter. They've also taught us that a guy who can't fill out his lineup can beat the best team in the league.

Study your projections. Check the injuries. Make your decisions. Somewhere Sunday, somebody will do something catastrophically stupid, and the other nine will spend the next decade reminding him.

That's The Fellas.

The football ends Monday night. The bullshit never does.
