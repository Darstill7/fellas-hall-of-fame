Why are some report-card categories N/A?

Waiver records cover selected pickups rather than every possible move. Start/Sit hindsight does not establish which choices were reasonable with the information available before games. Those categories are left unrated rather than assigning misleading skill grades.

Report-card methodology and coverage

Championships (20%) and verified runner-up finishes (8%) use certified title-game outcomes. Each result receives a recency factor of 0.25 + 0.75 × 0.5^((2025 − year) / 3.5), so old championships retain value while new success matters more. Playoff qualification (12%) uses all ten verified ESPN and Sleeper championship brackets, 2016–2025, with a 3.5-season recency half-life and an eligibility-based denominator. Only championship-bracket teams are counted; consolation games are excluded. Missing postseason data is never treated as missed playoffs.

Drafting (12%): percentile rank of average measured post-draft starter-point surplus from nonkeeper picks, using the documented 2020–2025 draft engine. Requires at least 12 scored picks. This is outcome value, not proof of draft process.

Trading (13%): percentile of mean recorded direct-point edge per verified, nonprovisional trade side; four verified trade sides minimum. This measures realized direct outcomes, not counterfactual trade quality.

Winning (10%): smoothed career regular-season win percentage. All-play (10%): smoothed rate of outscoring same-week opponents, reducing schedule-luck distortion.

Waivers (5%) and Start/Sit (5%): Not graded. Pending comprehensive completed additions, eligible alternatives and weekly legal roster/bench coverage. The displayed provisional total reweights only graded categories, so it must not be compared as a final 100-point grade.

Minimum evidence: 12 draft picks / four verified trade sides. A category below its threshold displays N/A. All ratings are source-model estimates through the archived completed 2026 Week 8 snapshot.
