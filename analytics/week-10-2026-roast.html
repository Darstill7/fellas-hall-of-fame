2026 Season / Week 10 / The Weekly Roast

The Week Merit Died

Stover gets fucked. Tony gets away with murder. And Kuhl trades his way back to the same quarterback.

THE FELLAS EDITORIAL DESK · NOVEMBER 17, 2026 · FINAL RESULTS

There are certain principles upon which fantasy football is built. Scoring points is good. Starting healthy players is preferable. Acquiring talent generally helps you win.

In Week 10, The Fellas took those principles, wiped their collective ass with them, and set the evidence on fire.

Stover scored 163.98 points and lost. Tony scored 89.52 and won. Kuhl traded away Nico Collins to reacquire a quarterback he'd already traded away once, watched that quarterback get injured, and finished with 48.92 points.

Meanwhile, Birch nearly doubled his Week 9 score, Stew transformed from a team that barely cracked 87 into a 161-point juggernaut, and Mitch's once-perfect season has suddenly developed a drinking problem.

Let's investigate this shitshow.

01 / Game of the week

The Greatest Performance Nobody Will Remember

Birch 178.32 · Stover 163.98

Last week, Stover defeated undefeated Mitch while leaving George Pickens inactive in his lineup.

This week, Stover apparently discovered the lineup button.

Christian McCaffrey: 46.30. George Pickens: 38.00. Trevor Lawrence: 26.78. Rams defense: 20.00.

A combined 131.08 points from four starters. A legitimate championship-caliber performance. And absolutely none of it fucking mattered.

Because Birch, who scored a miserable 87.06 last week, apparently spent the intervening seven days injecting his roster with experimental horse steroids.

Patrick Mahomes delivered 35.42. Chuba Hubbard exploded for 31.70. Cam Skattebo contributed 25.70. James Cook added 22.70.

Final score: 178.32–163.98. Combined: 342.30 points.

Here's where the historical research gets particularly cruel. In Week 11 of 2017, Stover recorded the league's second-highest score and lost to Birch. Nine years later, Birch has done it to him again.

Stover's 163.98 is the second-highest losing score in our verified historical comparison, trailing only Stew's 164.80-point loss to Mitch in Week 10 of 2025.

Different decade. Same opponent. Same fucking result.

Stover beat the undefeated team with 102 points, then lost with 164. This isn't fantasy football anymore. It's an elaborate psychological experiment, and Stover is the rat.

02 / The transaction desk

The Rich Get Richer, and Stew Opens a Quarterback Rental Business

Stew 161.40 · Mitch 134.04

Two weeks ago, Mitch was 8–0. Now he's 8–2, and the league's former untouchable juggernaut has lost consecutive games to Stover and Stew.

This week wasn't even a collapse. Josh Allen scored 22.34. Jaxon Smith-Njigba delivered 28.10. Parker Washington added 25.70. Isaiah Likely contributed 23.20. Mitch scored 134.04, enough to beat six other teams.

Unfortunately, Stew had decided to commit a series of violent acts against the scoreboard. Jahmyr Gibbs: 41.70. Emmett Johnson: 31.10. Chase Brown: 24.30. Nico Collins: 19.90. Those four combined for 117 points.

And that last name deserves special attention.

On September 30, Kuhl traded Drake Maye and Christian Watson to Stew for Jalen Hurts and Ladd McConkey. Then, on November 15, Kuhl decided he wanted Maye back. The price? Nico Collins.

Stew essentially operated a seven-week quarterback rental service and collected an elite wide receiver as the return fee. Collins promptly scored 19.90 in Stew's 161-point demolition of the league leader.

Stew went from scoring 87.36 in Week 9 to 161.40 in Week 10. That's a 74.04-point improvement.

Apparently, the secret to winning fantasy football is simply conducting business with Kuhl.
03 / Crime scene of the week

A Double Homicide Against Competence

Tony 89.52 · Kuhl 48.92

There is no reasonable way to explain this matchup without involving law enforcement.

Tony scored 89.52 points. Not 189.52. Eighty-nine. Brock Bowers contributed 2.80. His two starting running backs combined for 11.90. Dak Prescott's 34.42 points accounted for more than a third of the entire team's production.

Tony would've lost to seven of the other nine teams. Instead, he won by 40.60 points. Tony is now 7–3. At some point, we need to investigate whether this man has a direct financial relationship with the scheduling algorithm.

But Tony's ongoing assault on statistical justice is only the secondary crime. The primary suspect is Kuhl.

Kuhl entered Week 10 at 0–9, already holding the league's worst verified start to a season. He then traded Nico Collins for Drake Maye.

To be fair, there is an actual strategy here. Maye was a 14th-round keeper in 2026 and carries a potential 13th-round cost next year. Kuhl's season is already a smoking crater, so acquiring a valuable future keeper is not inherently stupid.

Unfortunately, the universe apparently interpreted this trade as a personal challenge. Maye immediately got hurt and finished with 3.02 points. A.J. Brown scored 3.70. Tre Tucker scored 1.40. Audric Estime scored 1.60.

Kuhl's entire starting lineup combined for 48.92 points. Christian McCaffrey, by himself, scored 46.30 for Stover. One fucking running back nearly outscored Kuhl's entire organization.

And the funniest part? Kuhl originally drafted Drake Maye.

He drafted him, traded him to Stew, spent six weeks without him, and then surrendered Nico Collins to bring him home.

That's not roster management. That's losing custody of your own child and paying a ransom to get him back.

Kuhl falls to 0–10. Ten weeks. Zero wins. Forty-eight points. Somewhere, the Shitter of the Year trophy just started engraving itself.

04 / The playoff climb

Three Straight Wins and Another Public Disappearance by Scott

Stilly 122.86 · Scott 88.10

Stilly's season has quietly shifted from underachievement to legitimate playoff positioning. Three consecutive wins. A 6–4 record. Three premium receivers doing exactly what they're supposed to do.

Ja'Marr Chase delivered 29.60. Amon-Ra St. Brown scored 24.80. CeeDee Lamb added 16.70. Together, the trio produced 71.10 points, more than 80% of Scott's entire team total.

Scott, meanwhile, assembled 88.10 points through a collection of players who apparently believed Week 10 was optional.

Bryce Young scored 8.10. Ashton Jeanty managed 8.40. Denzel Boston and Corey Kiner combined for 4.60.

Scott is now 4–6. Stilly is 6–4. The difference between the two teams wasn't particularly complicated.

One roster has three elite wide receivers. The other has a collection of increasingly convincing reasons to start researching next year's draft.

05 / The comeback desk

The Hot Streak Cools, but the Damage Continues

JMid 119.46 · Thommy 107.32

JMid entered Week 10 coming off consecutive weekly scoring titles. Week 8: 141.68. Week 9: 148.74. He was chasing the league's historical three-week scoring-title streak.

That pursuit ended this week, but JMid still collected another victory. Joe Burrow exploded for 33.16, Tony Pollard added 19.90, and the Miami defense chipped in 16.

Thommy countered with Deebo Samuel's 25.40 and Jared Goff's 20.42, but it wasn't enough.

The more concerning development is what happened after last week's Puka Nacua injury nightmare. Puka scored 28 points in Week 9 before that late-game injury became part of Thommy's ongoing personal horror film. He wasn't in the Week 10 starting lineup.

Thommy is now 3–7, sitting near the bottom of the standings while watching his playoff prospects slowly get lowered into a wood chipper.

JMid, meanwhile, has climbed back to 5–5. His scoring-title streak is over. His comeback isn't.

09 / The final word

Welcome to The Fellas

Ten weeks into the season, The Fellas have reached a point where competence and success are apparently unrelated concepts.

Stover finally figured out how to set a lineup and was rewarded with one of the most brutal losses in league history. Tony continues to collect wins like a corrupt politician collecting campaign donations.

Stew discovered that Kuhl's roster operates like a liquidation sale. Mitch, once undefeated, has now lost twice in a row.

And Kuhl? Kuhl spent seven weeks completing a round-trip transaction involving his own quarterback, got three points for his trouble, and remains the only man in the league who could theoretically improve his roster by accidentally deleting it.

Somewhere, Birch is celebrating a 178-point masterpiece. Somewhere else, Stover is staring at 163.98 points, wondering what he did to deserve this.

And somewhere, Tony is probably wondering why everyone is making such a big deal about scoring points.

Where the points don't matter, the schedule is a war crime, and Kuhl is already on the clock for 2027.
