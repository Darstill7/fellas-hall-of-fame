## The five games
### Stilly 104.16, Thommy 84.56 — The family-sized bag of excuses
CeeDee Lamb (24.7), James Cook (16.2) and Houston's defense (17) dragged Stilly over the line. Chase and Tyson combined for 7.1. This wasn't an offensive masterpiece; it was a successful extraction. Thommy's seven bench players combined for two points, with six scoring zero. Puka Nacua, Quinshon Judkins and Rhamondre Stevenson were among those zeros. Jared Goff scored 20.36 and Malik Nabers 15, but the running backs combined for 13.1 and Juwan Johnson registered zero. Stilly recorded his twelfth victory over Thommy, the new league rivalry wins mark. At this point Thommy should be getting a loyalty card.

### Stew 142.26, JMid 97.80 — The writer eats the answer sheet
Jonah Coleman (32.9), Jahmyr Gibbs (25.8) and Chicago's defense (17) made a mockery of our JMid -1.5 line. Stew has won five straight and averaged 151.83 over the last two weeks. JMid actually got something from his deadline shopping: Emmett Johnson and Garrett Wilson combined for 27.7. Breece Hall added 17.5 and George Kittle 16.9. Unfortunately, the rest of the roster did not receive the memo. Stew's new acquisition Kenneth Walker scored nothing this week; his future health is a gamble, not a certainty. The scary part is Stew didn't need him.

### Tony 142.30, Scott 98.76 — Four hundredths of a point
Dak Prescott scored 35.9; Javonte Williams 22.6; Jameson Williams 18.3. Tony beat Stew to the scoring crown by 0.04. Jonathan Taylor contributed 4.7, which makes the championship reunion more of a ceremonial ribbon-cutting than a rescue mission. Scott got 27.4 from Ashton Jeanty and 19.1 from DJ Moore, but his bench included a zero from Bijan Robinson and his three lowest-scoring starters totaled six points. Scott now needs help and has a particularly awkward Thanksgiving opponent.

### Birch 127.06, Mitch 83.96 — The wheels are coming off
Chuba Hubbard (26), Patrick Mahomes (24.96) and Mike Evans (19.9) made Birch's afternoon comfortable. Amon-Ra added 12.2 in his first start after the trade. Birch even left Travis Kelce's 22.4 on the bench. Mitch had no points from bench stars Drake London or Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and Josh Allen managed just 12.16. From 8–0 to 8–3: the lead is gone on points, not the season. Indianapolis lost 26–3 to Houston on Thursday; Mitch's own favorite team's misery now has a fantasy companion.

### Stover 91.74, Kuhl 76.40 — Someone had to win
George Pickens led Stover with 20.3. Chase Brown's first start yielded 6.1. Kuhl got 17.2 from Ladd McConkey and 14 from Denver, but Kirk Cousins and his three starting running backs combined for 25.6. Travis Etienne's 8.6 on the bench instead of Kyle Monangai's 1.8 would not have flipped the result. Stover won with the fourth-lowest score in the league; Kuhl remains winless. Our -25.5 spread did not cover. Congratulations to Kuhl on ruining a prediction.

## Trade Desk: the championship rerun
On August 30, 2021, Tony acquired Jonathan Taylor, A.J. Brown and Corey Davis from Thommy for Tyreek Hill, Josh Jacobs and Javonte Williams. The acquired packages produced 414.5 and 333.1 raw starter points respectively. Tony also acquired Joe Mixon and CeeDee Lamb that season and won the championship. On November 18, 2026, Tony acquired Taylor again, this time for Justin Jefferson from Mitch. First-week returns: Taylor 4.7, Jefferson 10.2. Five years later Tony is trying to reboot his franchise's championship movie. It is a different roster, different season and different NFL offense; history is not a projection.

Stew sent Garrett Wilson and Emmett Johnson to JMid for Kenneth Walker. JMid's 27.7 immediate starter points versus zero for Walker underline the difference between a bubble team's need for points now and a contender's willingness to wait. Walker's recovery remains uncertain. Stew also acquired Matthew Golden for Chase Brown from Stover; Brown delivered 6.1, Golden zero starter points. Stilly exchanged Amon-Ra St. Brown for James Cook with Birch: Cook 16.2, St. Brown 12.2. Both managers addressed roster construction, not just name value. These are early raw starter returns, not final trade grades or adjusted value.
