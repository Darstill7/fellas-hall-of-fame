Everybody's a Genius Until Sunday

Five trades. A first-place showdown. And a league-wide collapse of common fucking sense.

By The Shitter · Senior Correspondent
November 19, 2026 · Approx. 6-minute read

On Sunday, Stew scored 161.40 points, beat first-place Mitch and improved to 7-3. By Wednesday, he'd made three trades. Most people celebrate that kind of victory with a beer. Stew responded by tearing apart his roster like a methhead looking for copper pipes.

Four trades were completed Wednesday alone. Amon-Ra St. Brown, Justin Jefferson and Jonathan Taylor changed fantasy teams. James Cook, Kenneth Walker and Chase Brown did too. The Fellas have reached the point in the season when everybody thinks they're a general manager and nobody should be permitted to operate heavy machinery.

Kuhl is 0-10. Tony continues winning despite an offense that occasionally resembles a missing-persons report. Stover just scored 163.98 points and lost. And Stew, fresh off beating the league's best team, apparently decided his greatest obstacle to a title was his own roster.

The deadline: Five trades, ten grades

Stew's three-trade bender

Take the transactions together. Stew sent away Drake Maye, Garrett Wilson, Emmett Johnson and Chase Brown. He received Nico Collins, Kenneth Walker III and Matthew Golden. This was a deliberate consolidation: fewer assets, more immediate starting talent, and one speculative receiver for later.

But there is a fairly significant flaw in the master plan. Walker is questionable with a groin injury, and the player positioned to benefit if he sits is Emmett Johnson — now on JMid's roster. Those two managers face each other this week. The trade market has written its own revenge movie.

Stilly and Birch: The first-round divorce

Stilly gets James Cook (A-); Birch gets Amon-Ra St. Brown (A). Winner: Birch, narrowly. Stilly already has Ja'Marr Chase and CeeDee Lamb, so Cook fills a serious running back need. Birch gets the stronger individual receiver asset while retaining Derrick Henry and Chuba Hubbard. The transaction makes sense for both. Birch wins value; Stilly buys balance.

“I scored 24.8 points and got traded. Apparently Stilly looked at three elite receivers and thought, ‘You know what this team needs? Less talent.’”Amon-Ra St. Brown • Fictional satire

Tony and Mitch: Arms dealers

Tony gets Jonathan Taylor (A); Mitch gets Justin Jefferson (B+). Winner: Tony. Tony is 7-3 while scoring just 1,092.82 points, the fewest among the six current playoff-position teams. Taylor remedies his weakest area. Mitch gives Josh Allen a new fantasy receiving partner beside Jaxon Smith-Njigba, although JSN is on bye this week. Tony addresses a weakness; Mitch reinforces a strength.

“Seven wins with that offense? Jesus Christ. I thought I was being traded to a contender, not being brought in to identify the body.”Jonathan Taylor • Fictional satire

Stover gets the running back

Stover gets Chase Brown (A); Stew gets Matthew Golden (C+). A 4-6 manager coming off a 163.98-point loss needs results immediately. Brown helps. Golden may yet become an excellent keeper-value story, but that's an offseason argument. Stover needs to survive November first.

Roast the Writer: Receipts and regret

Last week, this publication picked Mitch over Stew, 127-123. Stew won 161.40 to 134.04. That is not a narrow miss. That's showing up to the wrong wedding and delivering the best-man speech with confidence.

The predicted Birch–Stover final was roughly 118-115. The actual: 178.32-163.98. The two teams combined for 342.30 points. Our analyst apparently studied their lineups through a blindfold and then confidently published the results.

And Tony was projected to score 112. He scored 89.52 and still beat Kuhl, who managed 48.92 — a fantasy score that ought to come with a refund.

The Final Flush

Mitch has the record. Birch has the ceiling. Tony has the luck. Stilly has the balance. Stew has an ambitious plan and a questionable running back. Stover has a one-way ticket to the edge of sanity. Kuhl has a season that should be sealed in concrete for future generations to study.

The trades are real, the rivalries are personal, and the consequences will outlive this Sunday. Somewhere in this week's deals is a championship-winning decision. Somewhere else is a trade that will be mocked in this group chat until retirement.

Everybody's a genius until the toilet flushes.
