Everybody's a Genius Until Sunday.

Five trades. A first-place showdown. And a league-wide collapse of common fucking sense.

THE SHITTER / Senior Correspondent
November 19, 2026 · Approximately six minutes to read

On Sunday, Stew scored 161.40 points, beat first-place Mitch and improved to 7-3. By Wednesday, he'd made three trades. Most people celebrate that kind of victory with a beer. Stew responded by tearing apart his roster like a methhead looking for copper pipes.

Four trades were completed Wednesday alone. Amon-Ra St. Brown, Justin Jefferson and Jonathan Taylor changed fantasy teams. James Cook, Kenneth Walker and Chase Brown did too. The Fellas have reached the point in the season when everybody thinks they're a general manager and nobody should be permitted to operate heavy machinery.

Kuhl is 0-10. Tony continues winning despite an offense that occasionally resembles a missing-persons report. Stover just scored 163.98 points and lost. And Stew, fresh off beating the league's best team, apparently decided his greatest obstacle to a title was his own roster.

The deadline: Five trades, ten grades

Stew's three-trade bender

Take the deals together. Stew sent away Drake Maye, Garrett Wilson, Emmett Johnson and Chase Brown for Nico Collins, Kenneth Walker III and Matthew Golden. Four assets became three, with Collins expected to anchor a receiver spot and Walker penciled in alongside Jahmyr Gibbs. This is consolidation, not random button-mashing.

But Stew also sold real production: Brown scored 24.3 last week, Johnson erupted for 31.1, and Maye offers the type of keeper upside a winless Kuhl desperately needs. Stew is trading flexibility and future value for a tighter championship lineup. He doesn't need to win every deal separately. He needs the combined return to deliver in December.

The first stress test, though, could be brutal. Walker entered the week questionable with a groin injury, and Johnson—now on JMid's roster—could benefit if Walker misses time. Those managers face each other immediately. A trade can look brilliant on a chart and still cost you a November game. The trade market has written its own revenge movie.

Stilly and Birch: The first-round divorce

Stilly / Cook A-Birch / Amon-Ra AWinner: Birch

Stilly already has Ja'Marr Chase and CeeDee Lamb. Three elite receivers looked spectacular in a screenshot, but his running back group had too many uncertain weekly roles. Cook gives him a genuine RB anchor without dismantling a premium receiving corps.

Birch is buying a different advantage. Derrick Henry and Chuba Hubbard offer enough backfield cover to move Cook, while Amon-Ra raises the receiving ceiling and gives Birch another potential keeper centerpiece. I give Birch a narrow edge in standalone player value; Stilly has a compelling case that his actual starting lineup improved more. If Cook swings a playoff win, the grade becomes a fascinating argument rather than a verdict etched in stone.

“I scored 24.8 points and got traded. Apparently Stilly looked at three elite receivers and thought, ‘You know what this team needs? Less talent.’”Amon-Ra St. Brown • Fictional satire

Tony and Mitch: Arms dealers

Tony / Taylor AMitch / Jefferson B+Winner: Tony

Tony sits 7-3 despite scoring just 1,092.82 points, the fewest among the six teams in playoff position. Jonathan Taylor gives him a featured running back to complement Javonte Williams and a much clearer route to big weekly scores. That matters more than padding an already sturdy receiver room.

Mitch isn't being robbed. Justin Jefferson beside Jaxon Smith-Njigba could become a nightmare when Seattle returns from its bye, and Josh Allen provides a weekly scoring floor. But surrendering Taylor leaves Mitch leaning harder on D'Andre Swift, Bucky Irving and depth. Tony gets the higher grade for repairing a weakness; Mitch trades balance for a higher-upside receiving attack. Both are legitimate championship bets.

“Seven wins with that offense? Jesus Christ. I thought I was being traded to a contender, not being brought in to identify the body.”Jonathan Taylor • Fictional satire

Stover gets the running back

Stover / Brown AStew / Golden C+Winner: Stover

Stover is 4-6 after scoring 163.98 points and losing, the sort of result that makes people question the existence of a merciful God. Chase Brown gives him another credible starter next to Christian McCaffrey, and, more importantly, helps him fight for a playoff spot right now.

Stew gets Matthew Golden and a possible keeper-value story, but that's a future-facing bet from a manager who just went all-in on winning now. Golden could make this look clever next summer. Brown could win Stover a game Sunday. The immediate winner is obvious—even if the long-term accounting remains open.

Roast the Writer: Receipts and regret

Last week, this publication picked Mitch over Stew, 127-123. Stew won 161.40 to 134.04. That is not a narrow miss. That's showing up to the wrong wedding and delivering the best-man speech with confidence.

The predicted Birch–Stover final was roughly 118-115. The actual: 178.32-163.98. The two teams combined for 342.30 points. Our analyst apparently studied their lineups through a blindfold and then confidently published the results.

And Tony was projected to score 112. He scored 89.52 and still beat Kuhl, who managed 48.92 — a fantasy score that ought to come with a refund.

Matchup 01 / The receiver advantage

The number: Stilly has averaged 116.68 points to Thommy's 103.20, a 13.48-point gap before adjusting for this week's personnel. Matchup 02 / The revenge game

Stew (7–3) vs. JMid (5–5)

Editorial spread / totalJMid -1.5 / 228.5

THE PICK · JMid -1.5 and Over 228.5. The bet is on availability, not on JMid suddenly becoming the superior team over ten games. Stew made three trades; the new roster's first exam comes with the answer sheet on the other sideline.

Matchup 04 / Game of the week

Editorial spread / totalBirch -1.5 / 235.5

THE PICK · Birch -1.5 and Over 235.5. I give Birch the narrowest edge on available personnel. I'd be entirely unsurprised if Josh Allen steals the game for Mitch. A good book respects both possibilities.

The Final Flush

Mitch has the record. Birch has the ceiling. Tony has the luck. Stilly has the balance. Stew has an ambitious plan and a questionable running back. Stover has a one-way ticket to the edge of sanity. Kuhl has a season that should be sealed in concrete for future generations to study.

The trades are real, the rivalries are personal, and the consequences will outlive this Sunday. Somewhere in this week's deals is a championship-winning decision. Somewhere else is a trade that will be mocked in this group chat until retirement.

Everybody's a genius until the toilet flushes.
