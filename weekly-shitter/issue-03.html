Thanksgiving is a time to reflect on what we're grateful for.

Stew is grateful for seven consecutive wins. Mitch is grateful the season didn't end three weeks later than he apparently thought it did. Tony is grateful his opponents have spent most of the fall observing a voluntary scoring embargo. And Kuhl is grateful Sleeper doesn't charge per loss.

Eleven weeks into the season, three managers are tied for first, two brothers are preparing to ruin Thanksgiving dinner, and the defending champion is fighting for his postseason life. Meanwhile, Stew traded for a running back who can't run, Tony is trying to recreate a championship roster from 2021, and Mitch has gone from undefeated to needing a game against Kuhl to restore public confidence.

Happy Thanksgiving, you absolute degenerates. Welcome to The Weekly Shitter.

Trade Desk: the championship rerun

2021 championshipTony + Taylor
2026 deadlineTogether again

On August 30, 2021, Tony acquired Jonathan Taylor, A.J. Brown and Corey Davis from Thommy for Tyreek Hill, Josh Jacobs and Javonte Williams. Tony went on to win the championship (10–3 regular-season record, first-place postseason finish). On November 18, 2026, he got Taylor back from Mitch for Justin Jefferson. Apparently Tony found the old championship DVD in the basement and decided to film a sequel.

The original deal was made before the season. This one leaves Tony three regular-season games to get the band back together. Taylor's first contribution was 4.7 points; Jefferson scored 10.2. That's less a triumphant reunion concert than a soundcheck at an empty fairground. An 8–3 team can afford to wait, though, and Tony needs a credible December backfield more than another receiver.

“I was told this was a championship reunion. Nobody mentioned the opening act was 4.7 points.”Jonathan Taylor · Imaginary locker room · Fictional satire, not a real quotation

Meanwhile, Stew sent Garrett Wilson and Emmett Johnson to JMid for Kenneth Walker. Wilson and Johnson immediately scored 27.7 starter points; Walker contributed zero. Walker is reported out for Thanksgiving, per CBS Sports, and Johnson is expected to start again. Stew traded away the man currently doing Kenneth Walker's job for Kenneth Walker, who is currently unable to do Kenneth Walker's job. A remarkable piece of corporate restructuring.

“I didn't realize getting traded to Stew meant I'd be working remotely.”Kenneth Walker III · Imaginary locker room · Fictional satire, not a real quotation

Stover got Chase Brown for Matthew Golden. Brown scored 6.1, Golden supplied zero starter points. Stover is fighting for his life; Stew is apparently also running a summer internship program. Stilly and Birch swapped James Cook and Amon-Ra St. Brown, who delivered 16.2 and 12.2 respectively. Stilly finally bought a running back instead of collecting wide receivers like limited-edition Pokémon cards. Birch took an already competitive roster and added another reason for everybody to dislike him.

The Shitter's Sportsbook: Thanksgiving board

Five games. Five original pre-kickoff lines. Two brothers, two miserable Colts fans and a commissioner hoping the folding-chair guy doesn't steal his playoff momentum. The numbers below are the original published picks, retained for accountability; this revision expands the analysis rather than rewriting the bets.

MATCHUP 01 / The Folding Chair Fight

Stilly (7–4) vs. JMid (5–6)

Editorial spread / totalJMid -2.5 / 228.5
Columnist's scoreJMid 119–113

The number: Stilly averages 115.54 to JMid's 114.19. That's practically a coin flip before personnel. Cook, Lamb and Chase give Stilly the stars, but Gordon and two young FLEX options leave plenty of room for a Sunday afternoon identity crisis. JMid has Hall, Pollard and Johnson in the starting nine, plus Kittle. Johnson benefiting from Walker's absence is the reason to shade the line toward JMid.

What changes it: If Stilly's FLEX options produce or his elite receivers erupt together, the lean flips quickly. JMid needs his receivers to do more than stand next to the ball.

THE PICK · JMid -2.5 · Over 228.5

A 232-point forecast is only a slim over lean. The two teams have enough offensive talent to reach it, but this is not a high-confidence total.

MATCHUP 02 / The Championship Reunion Tour

Tony (8–3) vs. Thommy (3–8)

Editorial spread / totalTony -8.5 / 225.5
Columnist's scoreTony 119–108

The number: Tony averages 112.28 against Thommy's 101.50. Dak, Taylor, Javonte and the receivers give Tony the steadier starting lineup, but Bowers' ankle adds uncertainty. Thommy has Goff, Nabers, Higgins and Waddle, so there is an actual path to a competitive score. Whether the running backs cooperate is another matter entirely.

What changes it: Bowers sitting would reduce Tony's advantage. A strong Goff and receiver showing could keep Thommy within a touchdown of the spread.

THE PICK · Tony -8.5 · Over 225.5

The forecast clears the total by only 1.5. This is the weakest over of the week, not a claim that Tony has suddenly become the 1999 Rams.

MATCHUP 03 / The Arms Race

Stew (8–3) vs. Birch (7–4)

Editorial spread / totalBirch -2.5 / 244.5
Columnist's scoreBirch 126–120

The number: Stew averages 126.73; Birch 118.48. By season scoring Stew deserves respect, but Birch has Mahomes, Henry, Amon-Ra, Rice and McBride on the same starting roster. That's an obscenely expensive Thanksgiving grocery bill. Walker is reported out; Coleman and Gibbs keep Stew dangerous, and Collins is in the FLEX. I'm narrowly favoring Birch's available personnel, not betting against seven weeks of history for fun.

What changes it: A repeat of Coleman's Week 11 explosion or Gibbs going nuclear makes Birch's slight edge disappear. This is the matchup of the week for a reason.

THE PICK · Birch -2.5 · Over 244.5

The combined season baseline is about 245.21. Both have a path to 120-plus; the total is close to fair.

MATCHUP 04 / The Brother Bowl

Stover (5–6) vs. Scott (4–7)

Editorial spread / totalStover -14.5 / 216.5
Columnist's scoreStover 119–101

The number: Nothing says Thanksgiving like sending your brother to the consolation bracket over the mashed potatoes. Stover averages 109.14 to Scott's 98.89. Add McCaffrey, Brown and Kyren in the starting nine and the personnel edge is real. Scott has Jeanty and DJ Moore, but is currently starting two tight ends. His Week 11 98.76 was respectable enough to lose by 43.54.

What changes it: Stover's running back availability is the swing factor. A Jeanty eruption could make dinner substantially more awkward for the favorite.

THE PICK · Stover -14.5 · Over 216.5

The season baseline is lower than the line; I'm leaning over because Stover's current lineup has more scoring potential than his season average. Low confidence.

MATCHUP 05 / The Colts Misery Bowl

Mitch (8–3) vs. Kuhl (0–11)

Editorial spread / totalMitch -36.5 / 210.5
Columnist's scoreMitch 130–84

The number: Mitch averages 125.01 and Kuhl 81.11. That's 43.90 points of separation before adjustments. JSN and London are off bye, Jefferson is in the lineup, and Josh Allen is still Josh Allen. Kuhl counters with Barkley and McConkey, plus Denver. For once, one of these Colts fans may have something to celebrate. Unless they tie, in which case the league should build a museum exhibit.

THE PICK · Mitch -36.5 · Over 210.5

The predicted total is 214. A returning Mitch lineup supports the over, but Kuhl's scoring floor makes this a fragile pick.
