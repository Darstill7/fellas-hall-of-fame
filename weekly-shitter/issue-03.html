There are three weeks left on the projected regular-season calendar, three managers sitting at 8–3, and one man still waiting to discover what the W column does. Thanksgiving is coming, the trade deadline has passed, and the Fellas have somehow managed to turn a family holiday into a hostile corporate acquisition.

Tony has gone back to the championship playbook. Stew has won seven consecutive games and is assembling a December roster while his November roster is already setting fire to the league. Mitch has lost three straight. Kuhl has lost eleven. Both root for Indianapolis. At some point this stops being a coincidence and starts looking like a subscription service.

A Tale of Two Assholes

Mitch was 8–0. Stew was 1–3. Now both are 8–3, with Stew ahead on points. Mitch lost to Stover, Stew and Birch. The championship conversation has changed faster than Kuhl can find a competent quarterback. Mitch still ranks second in scoring and gets Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Drake London back from bye. Fortunately, he plays Kuhl this week. If that doesn’t fix things, nothing will.

The completed matchup-by-matchup roast belongs to The Tuesday Hangover. Here, the question is what those results mean for the championship race.

Stew has gone from 1–3 to 8–3 with seven consecutive victories in Weeks 5–11. His last two scores average 151.83. The Shitter's original pick against him was a spectacular failure; the receipts are below.

From the editorial complaints desk: Stew identified the inaugural Hangover's incorrect five-game streak claim. His correction is valid: seven straight. The league's hottest manager has apparently added unpaid fact-checker to his job description. We sincerely apologize for depriving Stew of two opportunities to congratulate himself. Both seven and zero — Walker's Week 11 starter contribution — have been double-checked for his convenience.

Trade Desk: the championship rerun

2021 championshipTony + Taylor
2026 deadlineTogether again

On August 30, 2021, Tony acquired Jonathan Taylor, A.J. Brown and Corey Davis from Thommy for Tyreek Hill, Josh Jacobs and Javonte Williams. Tony went on to win the championship that season (10–3 regular-season record, first-place postseason finish). The original transaction and championship are verified in Sleeper's 2021 records. On November 18, 2026, Tony acquired Taylor again, this time for Justin Jefferson from Mitch. First-week returns: Taylor 4.7, Jefferson 10.2. Five years later Tony is trying to reboot his franchise's championship movie. It is a different roster, different season and different NFL offense; history is not a projection.

There is a tactical difference worth noting. In 2021 Tony bought three players before the season began; this time he has exchanged a single elite receiver for a running back with only three projected regular-season weeks left. He has less time to recover from a bad deal, but an 8–3 record gives him room to prioritize the postseason. Mitch, meanwhile, is trying to stabilize an offense that has lost three straight. Both are shopping for December, not just a prettier Week 11 box score.

Stew sent Garrett Wilson and Emmett Johnson to JMid for Kenneth Walker. JMid's 27.7 immediate starter points versus zero for Walker underline the difference between a bubble team's need for points now and a contender's willingness to wait. Walker is reported out for Thanksgiving, per CBS Sports; Emmett Johnson is expected to start again. Stew traded away the man currently doing Kenneth Walker's job for Kenneth Walker, who is currently unable to do Kenneth Walker's job. A remarkable piece of corporate restructuring. Stew also acquired Matthew Golden for Chase Brown from Stover; Brown delivered 6.1, Golden zero starter points. Stilly exchanged Amon-Ra St. Brown for James Cook with Birch: Cook 16.2, St. Brown 12.2. Both managers addressed roster construction, not just name value. These are early raw starter returns, not final trade grades or adjusted value.
