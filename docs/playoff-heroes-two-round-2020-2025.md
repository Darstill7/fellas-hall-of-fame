## Editorial takeaways grounded by receipts

- **2020, Koble:** David Johnson supplied 46.20 across both recovered championship-run starting lineups. Ryan Tannehill's 37.02 came in the semifinal only; the final lineup was different.
- **2021, Tony:** Davante Adams (41.80) led the two-round run, while Joe Mixon (29.50) led the championship game itself. Distinguish *playoff run leader* from *final-game leader*.
- **2022, Stover:** Patrick Mahomes contributed 48.78 across the two recovered games, leading both the semifinal and final starting lineups for that champion.
- **2023, Stilly:** Josh Allen contributed 44.14 over the two games. Mike Evans, acquired in the verified October 26 trade, added 32.60 across those same starts (24.10 semifinal, 8.50 final). The scoring receipts support a postseason contribution, not a claim that the trade alone decided the championship.
- **2024, Thommy:** Tee Higgins led with 50.40, including 34.60 in the title game; Jameson Williams contributed 46.50, including 24.50 in the razor-thin semifinal. Travis Kelce's verified 2024 postseason starts total 23.90 (5.50 semifinal, 18.40 final).
- **2025, Scott:** Bijan Robinson contributed 65.70 across both games. Brock Purdy's 36.92 comes from **one** verified title-game start, so he must not be presented as a two-start leader.

## Publication safeguards

1. Label this a **two-game champion-only sample**. A top score is not necessarily an objective playoff MVP across all league participants.
2. Preserve the difference between a one-start spike and two-start consistency.
3. Verify all participant identities and trades by the archived year's actual owner; JMid did **not** own Evans's 2023 seat.
4. Do not compare this metric to ESPN 2016–2019 or to teams without comparable starter boxscore evidence.
5. If an article uses `MVP`, identify whether it means the title game's highest scorer, the two-game champion's points leader, or an explicitly editorial award.
