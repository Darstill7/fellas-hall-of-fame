# The Fellas editorial standard
## Purpose
Professional sports journalism, polished presentation, and unprofessional league banter. Write for ten fantasy managers, not a statistics department. The joke should be specific enough that it could only belong to this league.
## Voice by destination
- Championship stories: prestige sports feature first; affectionate irreverence, earned callbacks, one or two sharp lines per chapter. Celebrate the winner without treating opponents as props. Never joke about real injury, medical emergencies, bereavement or other personal vulnerabilities.
- Manager bios: recognizable personality, career arc, defining wins and losses, recurring league-specific jokes. Roast documented fantasy decisions, not private life or identity. Do not fabricate quotes, motivations or manager reactions.
- Trade Museum: investigative journalism; identify who received what, actual starter production, date and scoring cutoff. Raw points are not an all-in trade verdict. Sarcasm is welcome when the evidence earns it.
- Hall of Shame: savage but accurate; distinguish official last-place awards, measured outcomes and subjective editorial plaques. Vulgarity is welcome when it improves a punchline.
- Player Hall and season retrospectives: nostalgic, witty, evidence-based; distinguish editorial selections from measured rankings.
- Tuesday Hangover and Weekly Shitter: unfiltered, roast-first, timely; existing published editions are explicitly out of scope for retroactive rewriting.
- Records, rules, standings and navigation: plain language; no jokes that obscure a number or definition.
## Publication checklist (required)
1. Verify every score, date, opponent, player attribution, streak, trade direction, playoff format, award and claim of a record against the relevant canonical data. Check arithmetic and coverage independently.
2. Confirm scope: season vs postseason, single week vs aggregate, current cutoff, original-manager starter points vs raw totals. Keep material caveats near claims; consolidate repetitive caveats in source notes only when meaning is preserved.
3. Lead with the story, not the methodology. Specific headline, strong opening, readable paragraphs, varied sentence lengths, earned ending. Avoid formulaic AI banter, gratuitous profanity, fake quotes and generic metaphors.
4. Give everyone a fair turn, including the editor's own team. Never invent an incident, rivalry, motive, quote or reaction to make a joke work. Distinguish subjective opinion, hypothetical counterfactuals and verified results.
5. Preserve the source links, numerical data, historical qualifications and editorial independence. For the 2022 canceled NFL game, handle the real-world circumstances respectfully; do not speculate about alternate outcomes.
6. Review on mobile: title, subhead, first paragraph, section breaks, links and optional evidence. A reader should understand the story without opening a spreadsheet.
7. Before publishing, run relevant canonical, editorial and browser checks, inspect rendered examples, and record any unresolved factual uncertainty. A passing automated test is not a substitute for a human-style editorial read.
## Change control
Keep factual corrections separate from stylistic edits. Preserve published weekly editions unless the commissioner explicitly approves a factual correction. Do not claim a complete audit until every named article has been read and checked. Record per-article review scope and outcome.
